*e2e-gauntlet lane · 2026-08-17 · Lee's desk · the round that walked Chapter 1's build*

---

## ① THE GOAL (Zach, 8/17, verbatim)

> "Run in auto mode, do not stop. Build this entire thing, and it should be walked end to end twice
> with a bunch of different scenarios logged in with real codes, real text."

Chapter 1 provisioned the two TEST accounts and shipped the agent-side panel. This chapter is the
other half: **does a real person, holding a real phone, actually get through the front door and find
their home search waiting for them?** Nothing here is a fixture. Every login used a code that
arrived as a text message on a real handset.

## ④ VERIFICATION — what was checked, not assumed

Every claim was verified at the layer that can actually disprove it, not at the layer that is
convenient:

- **the code** — read from the Twilio Messages API, not from a log line
- **the email** — read out of the real Workspace mailbox, with its INBOX label, not from a send record
- **the tour** — the appointment row read back from `hl_appointments` by id, and the text read back
  from Twilio; the id and the SMS were cross-checked against each other
- **the notes** — read back from `hl_home_tour_stop` with `feedback_by_lead_id` and `feedback_at`
- **the state engine** — `fetch('/api/my/myli-state')` executed inside the page, in the same call
  that read the DOM, so the API answer and the rendered sentence could not drift apart between reads

Every measurement also asserted its own `location.href` and `innerWidth` in the same call, because
the browser is shared with other lanes — a habit that immediately paid for itself when a new tab
silently reset the viewport from 1280 to 1440.

## ⑤ WHAT THE WALK FOUND

The round is **not** a clean pass, and the honest headline is that the front door works and the
filing underneath it does not. Eight defects were logged with reproductions; the full log is at
`.claude/reports/e2e-gauntlet/DEFECTS.md`. The two that matter most:

**Two people share one note row.** Conner wrote a note on a home at 17:50:30. Jasmeet wrote her own
note on the same home at 17:56:58. Both writes landed on the *same* database row, so the second
one silently destroyed the first — no warning, no merge, no history. And neither note was ever
visible to either of them, because the row notes are written to is not the row the dashboard reads.

**A signed-in client's tour filed on a different lead.** Jasmeet's tour request created its
appointment against a duplicate lead record that happens to share her phone number, rather than the
lead she was signed in as. Her own dashboard will never show that tour, and the agent following the
text lands on a record with no email address.

Both have the same shape underneath, and it is a shape the estate already has a law for: one real
tour exists in the database as **two appointment rows, one per person**, instead of one fact with a
seat for each participant. The overwrite, the invisible note and the misfiled tour all fall out of
that duplication — which is precisely what the seat model exists to prevent.
