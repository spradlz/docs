## What this section is

**Transaction Coordination (TC Loop)** is the HouseLoop CRM section for running a deal contract-to-close.
It is **our own dotloop, built and owned end-to-end** — documents, forms, fill, self-hosted e-signature,
the compliance archive, and a direct brokerage push all live on our side. It is a *light*, AI-native
(Myli-fillable) version of a dedicated TC tool, built on HouseLoop's own rails and reusing the CRM's
existing components.

Where [Website Integration](/guides/listing-lifecycle) is about the *listing* and the CRM guides are about
the *lead*, **Transaction Coordination is about the deal** — the contract-to-close record.

## The model

| Layer | Source of truth | HouseLoop role |
|---|---|---|
| **Listing** (the home) | `Listing` (web DB) | renders `/home/<slug>`, value, history |
| **Lead** (the person) | `hl_leads` (CRM DB) | CRM, AI follow-up, activity log |
| **Loop** (the container) | **`hl_loop`** (CRM DB) — ⚠ NOT YET BUILT | one property + one side; holds the documents |
| **Transaction** (the deal) | **`hl_transactions`** (CRM DB) | the deal record — written by disposition |

**The transaction record already exists: `hl_transactions`** (disposition-driven — marking a lead
*Under Contract* or *Closed* in the pipeline writes a row with address · price · closing · commission).
**Past clients = leads with a `closed` transaction.**

<Warning>
**⚠ A LOOP IS NOT A TRANSACTION — and the loop table does not exist yet** (Zach, 2026-08-06). This doc
previously treated them as one row; they are two things.

A **LOOP is a container for one property and one side**, holding every document for it. It exists **from
the first tour**, whether or not a deal ever happens — *"one client could have a loop for every house
they toured or wrote an offer on or closed."* Most loops never become transactions.

**The measurement that proves it:** `hl_contract_fill` has **21 rows — `lead_id` on 21/21,
`deal_id` on 0/21**. Every signed buyer agency belongs to a PERSON and not one belongs to a transaction,
because the business signs people before deals. That empty column is the missing table, visible in data.

**And the schema cannot represent the common case today:** `hl_tc_loop_document.transaction_id` is
`NOT NULL`, so a house someone toured — or wrote a losing offer on — has real paperwork and nowhere to
put it.

**ONE LOOP CAN PRODUCE MANY TRANSACTIONS** (a duplex or a portfolio — rare, but real), so the pointer
runs `hl_transactions.loop_id → hl_loop`, never a single `transaction_id` on the loop. A column holds
one value; "very rare" is not "never", and a schema that cannot express the rare case is wrong rather
than simplified.

**A document carries BOTH pointers** — *"documents are related to the transaction and are held inside
the loop."* `transaction_id` (related to) **and** `loop_id` (held in). Never a choice between them.

Spec: `.claude/reports/the-loop-model-spec.md` · board items **154–158**. Builds after pour-one
acceptance. **No legacy pour** — the 13,449 dotloop loops stay in the workings estate as history.
</Warning>

The child tables that make up the loop file: `hl_tc_loop_folder` (the **sections** of a loop) /
`hl_tc_loop_document` (**our document source of truth**), `hl_transaction_participant` (people — 3,206
rows), `hl_tc_loop_task` (per-loop task instances), `hl_transaction_milestone` (the dates — renamed from
`hl_tc_loop_event` on 2026-08-06 because it FKs to `hl_transactions` and never had a `loop_id`),
`hl_contract_template` / `hl_contract_fill` (the 5 blank forms + a signed document — ⚠ `contract_fill`
is misnamed; "fill" is the mechanism, not the thing, rename pending Zach's word), `hl_tc_email`.

## ⚙️ HOW A LOOP IS RUN — the two dials (WORKED OUT 2026-07-31 · schema LOCKED)

<Info>
**Schema lives in [Transaction & Loop Model](/schema-lock/transaction-model)** — table names, columns,
constraints, migration sequence. This guide is the OPERATING story: what Ana sees, what she may change, and
what happens when she changes it. It never restates schema.
</Info>

Every loop carries **two dials that answer different questions**. One field could never hold both — proven
on a live loop the morning of 2026-07-31, when a signed showing agreement produced a status that did not
match the stage.

| | **Loop status** — *where is the deal?* | **TC status** — *what does this file need?* |
|---|---|---|
| Who moves it | **derived** from the stage's section while the deal is alive — **set by hand** when it ends without closing | **the coordinator**, by hand |
| Ana sees | a read-only chip | a dropdown she owns |
| Values | Pre-Contract · Pre-Listing · Under Contract · Closed · *the exits* | Processing · Paused/Blocked · Waiting for Closing · Complete |
| Before a TC is assigned | already correct | **empty** — then `Processing` on assignment |

<Warning>
**Loop status is DERIVED while the deal is alive and EXPLICIT when it dies.** Those are two different
mechanisms and it matters:

```
DERIVED   pre_contract · pre_listing · under_contract · closed
          Follows the stage's section. Nobody touches it. It cannot drift.

EXPLICIT  Listing Not Won · Offer Not Accepted · Fell Through
          Listing Expired · Listing Cancelled · Archived
          Ana SETS it, and it OVERRIDES the derivation permanently.
```

Nothing can compute "we lost the listing" from a stage — a lost listing looks identical to one still being
worked until a human says otherwise. So the field is nullable: **empty means derive from the stage; set
means a person ended this loop on purpose**, and the loop stops following the pipeline from that moment.
</Warning>

**Read them together and the loop tells its whole story:**

```
Under Contract   +  Waiting for Closing   deal is live, Ana's work is done, waiting on the date
Closed           +  Processing            house closed, still chasing commission docs + compliance
Pre-Contract     +  Processing            buyer agreement out for signature, Ana chasing it
Archived         +  Processing            contract died, still releasing earnest money
```

<Note>
**Why "Waiting for Closing" is not the same as the Clear-to-Close stage.** Clear to Close is the *lender*
clearing the file. Waiting for Closing is *Ana* saying "nothing left for me until closing day." Two facts
about the same loop, and deleting either one loses information a coordinator needs.
</Note>

## 🚪 WHEN A DEAL DIES — the exits (SEQUENCED)

A loop is **one attempt at one deal**. Lawrence Guenther's listing is a loop. You did the presentation, you
did not win it — that loop ends, permanently, with every document in it. Lawrence is still Lawrence, still
in the pipeline, and if he calls next spring **that is a new loop**.

That is why a dead deal is a loop status and never a stage: *a lead has one stage; a lead has many loops.*
One buyer can carry four archived loops and a fifth that closes, and their pipeline position never moved
backwards.

| Ana picks | Side | Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Listing Not Won** | seller | did the presentation, did not get the listing |
| Listing Expired | seller | we had it, term ran out |
| Listing Cancelled | seller | we had it, seller cancelled |
| Offer Not Accepted | buyer | wrote the offer, seller took another |
| Fell Through | both | went under contract and died — inspection, financing, appraisal |
| Archived | both | the plain catch-all — buyer changed their mind, went quiet |

One dropdown, one chip: the reason **is** the status, so there is no second field to leave blank.

<Warning>
**TODAY there is no exit at all.** The seller pipeline runs Listing Appointment Met → Listing Agreement
Sent → Listing Agreement Signed with nothing in between for a loss. Lawrence Guenther has **nowhere to be
dispositioned right now**, and listing-presentation win rate cannot be computed. `listing_not_won` is
sequenced EARLY for exactly this reason — it is purely additive.
</Warning>

## 🧩 ADDING A STAGE, A SECTION, OR A STATUS — no engineer (PARTIALLY SHIPPED)

**Shipped 2026-07-31 (migration 0531):** sections are rows. Labels, colors and icons used to live in a
TypeScript file, so renaming "Working the Buyer" needed a deploy. Now it is one row, edited in
`tc/configure/stages`.

Every section declares **who may edit the stages inside it**:

| Section | Managed by | What Ana sees |
|---|---|---|
| Working the Buyer · Win the Listing · List Prep · Nurture | `pipeline` | edit stages inline |
| **Under Contract → Close** | **`tc_milestones`** | **read-only, with a link: *Managed in TC → Milestones*** |

The TC owns Under Contract → Close because **the TC owns the dates.** Those milestones carry anchors,
offsets, business-day math and holiday rolls, and 139 task templates hang off them. No other section has
any timing to configure — which is why it is the only one that redirects.

Adding something new is a row, in the matching configure page:

```
a new SECTION   → tc/configure/stages       label, color, icon, order, managed_by
a new STAGE     → tc/configure/stages       inside a `pipeline` section
a new MILESTONE → tc/configure/milestones   the anchor + offset it computes from
a new TASK      → tc/configure/tasks        the milestone it hangs off + its offset
a LOOP STATUS   → config row                its side, and its group (active/closed/archived)
a TC STATUS     → config row                the coordinator's dropdown
```

## 📅 MILESTONES AND TASKS — one hierarchy, not two (SEQUENCED)

**Ana's ruling, 2026-07-31: a task belongs to a milestone.** Not to a phase.

The data already agreed — all 139 task templates were anchored to a milestone date, and `phase` was a
second grouping on top saying nothing the anchor did not already say. It made 81 tasks on one loop look
like they lived in two hierarchies at once. **Phases are retired.**

```
MILESTONE  (a dated event)
   └── TASKS  (due = the milestone's date ± an offset)
   └── advances the STAGE, where one applies
```

A loop carries roughly **16 milestones and 81 tasks** — each milestone with its own date, its own tick, its
own who-and-when. That is why they are a checklist and not a single field: the TC overrides individual dates
constantly, because inspections extend and closings move.

Not every milestone owns a stage, and that is deliberate: **Funded** and **Possession** happen *after* the
deal closes, so the stage stays `Closed` while the dates keep landing. A pipeline cannot go past its own
end; a date list can.

`start` — the biggest bucket at 57 of 139 tasks — becomes a real milestone, **Loop Started**, so every task
nests under a milestone with no special cases.

<Warning>
**THE DATE ENGINE HAS NEVER BEEN SWITCHED ON.** Measured live 2026-07-31: **0 of 487 live tasks have a due
date**, and **0 of 30 milestones** are wired to advance a stage. The templates, anchors, offsets,
business-day rules and holiday rolls are all built. Nothing computes. **The machine is fully assembled and
has never been turned on** — which is why milestones and stages drifted into two hand-typed lists of nearly
the same words.

Related, and reassuring: the `is_active` toggle on a milestone is a **seed filter read once** when a loop is
created. It fires no automation and notifies nobody. Turning one off is safe.
</Warning>

## Own the whole stack — why (LOCKED 2026-06-27)

TC Loop is **our own dotloop**, owned end-to-end. We do NOT hand a third party our finished documents.
Two hard reasons a wrapped dotloop can't satisfy:

1. **dotloop can't fill or sign via API.** No API-fillable smart fields, and you can't place signature
   fields or trigger a signature through its public API (UI-only) — so Myli could never run a contract
   through dotloop.
2. **SkySlope / eXp compliance requires we own the documents end-to-end** — we must push signed docs
   directly to SkySlope, only possible if the documents live on our side.

**dotloop is OUT of the critical path.** It is an optional legacy importer only — read historical SRG
loops via the read-only API in `lib/dotloop/client.ts`. See [dotloop integration](/guides/dotloop-integration).

## E-signature = Documenso, self-hosted (the system of record)

**Documenso, self-hosted, is the system of record for signed documents** — the sealed final PDF plus its
completion certificate are archived to **our R2**. dotloop is only an optional legacy backup, out of the
critical path. This is not a plan: **e-sign shipped 2026-07-06** (instance live at `esign.houseloop.ai`;
multi-party signing — buyer / co-buyer / agent / broker — proven on all 3 contract forms; sealed PDFs
archived to R2; only the `DOCUMENT_COMPLETED` webhook terminates the flow). The send path is in
`apps/houseloop-crm/lib/esign/send-loop-document.ts`.

**The compliance bar.** US e-signatures are governed by the **ESIGN Act (federal) + UETA** (both adopted
by Kansas and Missouri). An e-signature binds an agency/purchase contract when we capture: (1) **intent to
sign**, (2) **consent** to do business electronically, (3) **attribution** (email/identity verify + IP +
timestamp), and (4) **integrity + retention** — a tamper-evident record (document hash / digital seal),
retained and reproducible. The legal backbone is the **audit trail / completion certificate** (who · when ·
IP · email-verified · doc hash · event log) plus the sealed final PDF. US real estate does **not** require
an RFC-3161 trusted-timestamp authority (that's EU/eIDAS) — a reliable system timestamp + audit log + seal
is the accepted standard.

**Documenso provides all of it** — audit log, PAdES cert-seal, completion certificate, and an API to place
fields, send, and webhook on completion — and is self-hostable, so we own the data and pay **$0 per
signature**. (Chosen over SignWell for ownership, no per-sig cost, and SkySlope-grade control.)

### The model — licensing lives on the USER

`hl_user_license` (migration `0509`), **one row per agent per state**:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `user_id` | FK to `hl_users`, cascades on delete |

<Warning>

</Warning>

## Forms — the Contract Engine

The **Forms** page turns the brokerage's flat dotloop contract PDFs into **fillable forms** HouseLoop

1. Each form's flat PDF + a coordinate **field-map** (PDF field → lead/deal data source) lives in

2. The engine (`lib/tc/fill-contract.ts`, pdf-lib) adds AcroForm fields and fills each from the lead (name,

The **showing-door** forms (the buyer journey): **Single Property Buyer-Broker Rep** (default, one house) →
**Exclusive Buyer Agency** (multiple homes). Disclosures (wire fraud, ABA, broker disclosure) roll in later

- **`{ formKey }` → Myli's headless path.** Fills straight from loop data (`createFillSend`).

See **The document workflow** below for how a draft becomes a filled, editable, signable document.

## The document workflow — Add → Fill form → Sign

Every document on a loop is editable through a **form**, not by clicking fields on the PDF (dotloop's clunky,
old-school way). The loop is Zach's: *"a form that we fill out that updates the pdf."*

1. **Add Document** (`AddFromFormModal`, sends `draft: true`) → a **blank DRAFT** row appears with a **Fill
   out** button. Nothing is pre-stamped; no junk defaults from other docs.
2. **The kebab** (⋮, on every doc row via `HlDropdown`) → **Edit fields · Open PDF · Delete document**. (The
   menu portals *above* the modal — z-index `10080` — so it's actually usable; a doc name is clickable to the
   same form.) Delete → `DELETE /api/tc/loops/[id]/documents/[docId]` (blocks `signed` docs with a 409).
3. **Fill out / Edit fields** opens the **fill form** (`FillReviewModal`) — every field editable, pre-filled
   from the loop (buyer name, general location, price range, term dates). Save → the doc walks
   **draft → filled**, a fresh PDF is stamped to R2, and the edited values land in `hl_contract_fill.values`
   (which drives the PDF). Re-openable and re-stampable any time via the same form.
4. **Send for signature** → the Documenso e-sign flow (below).

<Note>
**Signatures are anchored, never eyeballed.** Signature/initials coordinates are **extracted from each PDF**
(`tools/houseloop-contracts/anchor-sig-fields.mjs`) — box Y differs per page, so a single hardcoded number is
always wrong. The extractor writes the field maps in `lib/tc/field-maps/*.json`. Law:
[`.claude/rules/SIGNATURE_ANCHORING.md`](/guides/esignature) — *"read the box, don't guess it."*
</Note>

### The signature-request email — signature block, neutral body, and NO client-text leak (2026-07-24)

Three rules govern what each signer's Documenso email actually says (`lib/esign/send-loop-document.ts` +
`components/leads/detail/SendForSignatureModal.tsx`):

1. **The email body is NEUTRAL — the casual client text never leaks to the broker.** Documenso's `meta.message` is
   ONE message applied to EVERY signer (buyer + agent + broker). So the modal sends a professional message —
   *"Please review and sign the document. Once all parties have signed, everyone receives a completed copy…"* — as
   the e-sign email body. The agent's **casual note** (e.g. *"Trish will meet you at 1:30 😀"*) is sent SEPARATELY
   as the **client SMS only**, never as the Documenso email. (Before the fix the broker was getting the casual
   text in his signature-request email.)
2. **The sending agent's signature is appended.** `sendLoopDocument` resolves the agent's signature (name ·
   Spradling Group | HouseLoop · phone · email) from their dialer + connected inbox and appends it to BOTH the
   Documenso message and the logged thread copy (`logEmailSend`), so the request reads like a real email from the
   agent. Documenso renders plain text — a full graphic signature would require sending via Gmail instead.
3. **The signing email is logged to the Emails thread.** Documenso sends from its own SMTP, so the request would
   otherwise be invisible in the lead's Emails lens — `sendLoopDocument` records it via `logEmailSend` + an
   `email_sent` run-log row so it shows in the feed's Email card.

## The 3-layer engine + dynamic dates (A-Frame parity)

The stages, milestones, and tasks are an **EXACT MIRROR of A-Frame** today (names, phases, anchors, offsets,
assignees lifted verbatim from the recon). Only the UI chrome is ours. **Why:** mirror A-Frame 1:1 to
understand + measure it before changing anything — a divergence in task content is a bug, not a feature.
There are **no per-task instruction manuals** (A-Frame's task `description` is empty; verified). TC task rows
that read e.g. "SLACK: Front end TC accepts assigned transaction" / "AFRAME: Change Status" are the verbatim
A-Frame source text, not placeholder copy. Converting to a HouseLoop-native set is deferred.

Three layers + an offset engine:

1. **STATUS** — the headline word = our **Stages** (~5/side).
2. **DATES — 4 KEY DATES you ENTER drive everything:** `effective` (Under Contract) · `inspection_end` ·
   `closing` · `on_market` — the milestones' anchors.
3. **TASKS (139, in phase folders)** — each is a **relative rule** (`anchor + offset`). Buyer = Intake ·
   Contract Review · Scheduling · Communication · Compliance · Inspections · Appraisal · Closing. Listing
   adds the pre-market run-up: Within 24h of ERTS Signed · 10 Days Before Going Live · 5 Days Before Going
   Live · Listing Goes Live · Weekend After Going Live.

**Dynamic dates** (`lib/tc/date-engine.ts`): a task/milestone stores a RELATIVE rule; the due date computes
from the live key date and cascades when it moves. Rule = anchor (key date) or task→task chain · signed
offset · `day_unit` · `business_day_def` (TRID precise/general) · weekend/holiday roll · holiday calendar
(US_FEDERAL) · conditional "If Applicable" · `task_type`. Proven: 13/13 unit tests.

> **Status = headline · Milestones = the key dates · Tasks = the auto-scheduled checklist.** Enter 4 dates →
> the plan schedules; move one → it all re-shifts.

**Task counts (A-Frame parity):** 58 buyer + 81 listing = **139**. Listing's 81 = 57 contract-to-close +
24 pre-market.

### ⬜ PLANNED — the TEAMS table + admin UI (the last thing in this build)

**Zach 2026-08-03: _"we need a team table dude, that is how we build this correctly otherwise it's hidden in
typescript somewhere."_ He is right, and this is the deliberate debt we took to keep moving.**

**What shipped instead (2026-08-03):** `hl_role_team` (mig 0582/0583) — a role belongs to MANY teams, which
is the part the loop actually needed. `team_key` is a **hard-coded TEXT value**, not a foreign key: today the
legal values `sales` and `transaction` live in code, and adding `marketing` means a one-line migration.

| Role | Teams |
|---|---|
| Real Estate Agent | `sales` + `transaction` — the row a single-value column could never hold |
| Showing Partner · Lender | `sales` |
| Broker · Transaction Coordinator | `transaction` |
| AI ISA · AI Receptionist | `sales` — **AI is a TYPE, not a team** |
| AI Clone | *(none — a clone follows the person it clones, so its team is a property of the member)* |

**What still has to be built, and why it is not optional forever:**

1. **`hl_team`** — a real table: `key · label · description · sort · is_active`. Today the vocabulary is
   hard-coded, which means the list of teams is invisible to the database and un-editable by a human. That is
   exactly the "hidden in TypeScript" failure mode our own catalog laws exist to prevent.
2. **`hl_role_team.team_key` → FK** to that table, once it exists.
3. **A teams admin UI** — Zach's spec, modelled on the SureSend My-Teams cards: *create a team → name,
   description, members*. Lives under My Team.
4. **The department axis** — the list is department-shaped and will grow:
   `sales · transaction · marketing · recruiting · content` (+ `past_client` when the referral dashboard
   lands). Naming the first key `sales` rather than `lead` was chosen so it still reads correctly when
   marketing arrives.
5. **Per-team permissions** — Zach: *"need separate permissions for your teams."* Permission columns land on
   `hl_role_team` (Lee's ruling: that is why it is a join table and not a `text[]`). `hl_role.grants_tc`
   stays the permission bit until then, and folds in here.
6. **Retire `hl_role.team_group`** — the single-value column `hl_role_team` supersedes. `rename-readiness.mjs`
   measured **16 live reader/writer sites**; per `ONE_COMMIT_RENAME` all 16 move in the SAME commit as the
   data. Shipping half a rename is what caused the 7/28 outage, which is why this was NOT bundled into 0582.

**The rule that makes AI scale here** (Zach's open question, answered): filter on what a role can *do*, never
on `kind='ai'`. **A team says where you appear; `kind` says whether you can sign.** So a future AI Transaction
Coordinator joins the `transaction` team, appears on the loop, and holds `view_only` — while `can_sign` is
never offered to it, because a signature on a legal document must be a person. Blocking AI from loops outright
would have made that role impossible.
