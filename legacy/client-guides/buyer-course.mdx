## Where the idea came from

Two separate prompts landed on the same conclusion within a day of each other.

Ana, after an hour on the phone walking a buyer's wife through offer strategy:

> I feel we should make a cool video like the titans videos exposing the options to make it stronger.

And the observation that a single PDF handed over at the start is the wrong shape for the problem: a buyer
who has not yet fallen in love with a house does not care about earnest money, appraisal gaps or escalation
clauses. Handed everything at once, they are drinking from a fire hose.

## The proposition

Instead of one document delivered once, deliver a **short lesson at the moment it becomes relevant** —
each one a couple of minutes, tied to a stage the buyer has actually reached.

```
Appointment set
   → Welcome, meet your team
        → Why you sign a Buyer's Agency Agreement
             → What happens at your showing
                  → [ they find a house ]
                       → Offer strategy    ← the current guide starts HERE
                            → Under contract
                                 → Closing
```

The point of the ordering: **the existing buyer's guide is the fourth beat, not the first.** Everything in
it — the five-offer comparison, the six terms — only matters once emotion exists. Before that it is noise.

### The part that makes it worth building

The same lesson content serves three audiences, from one source:

| Audience | What they get |
|---|---|
| **Buyers** | The lesson, at the stage it matters |
| **Agents** | The identical lesson as training — new agents learn the method the team actually uses |
| **The AI** | The same material as knowledge, so Myli coaches consistently instead of improvising |

Improve a lesson once and all three get better. That is the argument for lessons over a document: not that
documents are bad, but that a document cannot be a training curriculum and an AI knowledge base at the same
time without drifting into three copies.

## Naming — settled

<Note>
**These are COURSES and LESSONS. They are not "Playbooks."**

That word is already taken and is load-bearing: `hl_playbooks` and `hl_playbook_runs` are real tables,
`/settings/playbooks` is a real page, and *"no playbook, no voice"* is signed law (Zach, 2026-07-27) — a
playbook is the AI's unit of authorization. Calling client training "playbooks" would make
*"which playbook is this lead in?"* ambiguous between an AI run and a training enrollment.

"Chapter" is also spoken for, by the chaptered lead and TC lifecycle model.
</Note>

## What already exists that we would reuse

The course tooling is **already built** — for a different brand. AI Titans has course and lesson
definitions, generation scripts, and publishing tooling, and the platform architecture is explicit that
this is a shared engine rather than an AI Titans possession:

> AI Titans is a brand, not a system. The tools are generic content engine processes that AI Titans
> happened to adopt first.

So HouseLoop would be a second brand on `content-engine`, not a fork.

**The honest gap:** that engine publishes to Skool and YouTube, and its lesson schema is built for video
scripts. It has **no per-student progress tracking and no in-app delivery**. A buyer-facing course inside
HouseLoop needs both. That is the real scope, and it is unestimated.
