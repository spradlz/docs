# ⭐ Kill the Two-Hop

**Milestone completed August 19, 2026.** [Floor 6 on the board](/master-plan/floor-6)

---

## What this is

This is the notification half of the same disease as [filing at capture](/milestones/filing-at-capture).
It is short on purpose, because the honest description of it is short.

A bell should ring when something happens. Ours rang when something happened *and then* a second,
slower thing also happened. The signal arrived, and then a background catch-up had to come along
behind it and carry the readable part across. Only then could the bell ring. Two hops instead of
one.

That catch-up was measured running nearly **nine minutes** behind. And for a stretch before this, the
timing was worse than merely slow: a waiting notification could sit until some *unrelated* later
piece of traffic happened to come through and nudge it along. Your bell for one client's message was
being timed by another client's activity.

## The written detail

There is no separate specification page for this one either. [The captain's manual](/fub/captain-manual)
names the old two-hop road and marks it condemned, which is the closest written record of what was
removed.

## The walkthrough

### The two hops, and why the second one existed

The second hop was never a design choice. It was the consequence of the message body living on a
different machine than the notice that a message existed. Something had to go and get it. That
"something" was the second hop, and everything downstream - the card, the bell - inherited its
timing.

### Killing it was not a separate build

This is the part worth saying plainly, because it is a genuinely good outcome rather than a corner
cut: **filing at the moment of capture removed the second hop entirely.** Once the message text
travels with the signal, there is nothing left for a catch-up job to fetch, so there is no second hop
to wait on.

Two problems, one cause. Fixing the one underneath closed this one at the same time. No separate
code, no separate risk, no second thing to maintain.

### What it measures now

The same live production run that proved the card: **5.4 seconds from signal to filed**, on the real
system, on August 19. The bell hangs off that same moment. The old bound - a relay running up to
**8 minutes 43 seconds** behind, plus the possibility of never arriving at all - is gone, because the
road it lived on is gone.

## What you can do with it

| What happens | What you get |
|---|---|
| A lead messages you | The bell is timed by the message, not by a background job's schedule |
| The background catch-up falls behind | Your notifications are unaffected - they no longer travel that road |
| The system is quiet for hours | Your bell still rings on time; it is not waiting for other traffic to push it |
