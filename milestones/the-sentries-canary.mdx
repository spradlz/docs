# ⭐ The Sentries and the Canary

**Milestone completed August 19, 2026.** [Floor 3 on the board](/master-plan/floor-3)

---

## What this is

A pipeline that breaks loudly is a nuisance. A pipeline that breaks *quietly* is the expensive kind -
nothing errors, no page turns red, leads simply stop arriving and nobody finds out for a week.

A **sentry** is a watchman posted on one road. It knows how much traffic that road normally carries,
and it pages when the road goes quiet. A **canary** is the other half: a harmless test signal we send
through the pipe on purpose, so that if the road is dead we find out from our own probe rather than
from a client who never got a call back.

The part of this that mattered most is unglamorous: **the numbers a sentry watches by now live in the
record, not in the code.** Changing "how quiet is too quiet" used to mean a developer and a deploy.
Now it is data.

## The walkthrough

### You open Systems Health

Every pipeline, scraper and outside dependency on one board.

![The systems health board showing one system down, four of six healthy, and the Follow Up Boss import flagged after thirteen hours without a success](/images/milestones/the-sentries-canary/the-sentry-board.png)

This screenshot is the board doing its job, not the board looking pretty. **1 system down, 4 of 6
healthy.** The open issue at the top names itself: sixteen failed runs in twenty-four hours. And down
in the probe cards, the Follow Up Boss import has gone amber with the reason written on it - *thirteen
hours since last success*.

That is the whole design. You do not have to know what healthy looks like; the board tells you what
it measured and what it expected.

### Each card names the fact it reads

A probe does not report a mood. It reads one specific fact - the timestamp of the last successful
run, the count of messages in the last week - and compares it to a limit that lives in the record. If
it cannot read that fact, it says *cannot measure*. It never rounds that up to *all clear*, which is
the failure mode that makes a monitor worse than no monitor.

### The limits are data

Deciding that a road is dead after thirteen hours rather than six used to be a code change. It is now
a value you can edit, per road, without a deploy - which means the thresholds can actually be tuned
against what each road really does instead of staying at whatever the first guess was.

## What you can do with it

| What you do | What happens |
|---|---|
| Open Systems Health | Every pipeline and dependency on one board, with a headline of how many are healthy |
| Read the red banner | The count of systems down, and the open issues that made them go down |
| Read a probe card | What it watches, the fact it read, and how far outside the limit that is |
| Change a limit | The threshold is a value in the record - no deploy, no developer |
| Watch the canary | A harmless test signal is pushed through the pipe on a schedule, so a dead road announces itself |
