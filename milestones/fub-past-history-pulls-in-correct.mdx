# ⭐ The Past History Pulls In Correct

**Milestone completed August 18, 2026.** [Floor 34 on the board](/master-plan/floor-34)

---

## What this is

We had a hole in our email history roughly seven weeks wide.

The thing that watches our inboxes had died. A slower path was still trickling some mail in behind
it, which is exactly why nobody noticed - the history was not empty, it was *thin*, and thin looks a
lot like quiet. From the outside it just seemed like a stretch where fewer people emailed us.

Eight mailboxes are now connected to live capture, and the whole back history has been replayed.
**7,789 messages recovered.**

## The walkthrough

### There is no screenshot on this page, on purpose

Every picture of recovered history in our files sits on a real client's record, showing their full
name, phone number and email address together. This page is shared with the whole team. A weaker
illustration is a better trade than publishing somebody's inbox, so this milestone is told in
numbers.

### Before: seven weeks of thin history

The watch that captures mail as it arrives had stopped. A slower per-mailbox path kept collecting a
trickle, so the gap never announced itself. Roughly seven weeks of correspondence was either missing
or arriving late.

### The part that actually mattered: how it was replayed

There were two ways to fix this, and the fast one was wrong.

The fast way is to read the old mail and write it straight into the database. It would have worked in
the sense that rows would exist. But those rows would never have been *seen* by the system - not
attached to the right people, not turned into cards, not given any of the treatment a message
arriving right now gets. You would have a store full of mail and records that still looked empty.

Instead the history was replayed **through the real writers** - the exact same code that files a
message arriving this second. Every recovered message got the identical treatment to a live one, so
it lands on the right person's record and reads like everything around it.

### And not one bell rang

Replaying nearly eight thousand old messages must not fire nearly eight thousand notifications at
people at two in the morning. That is the obvious disaster, and it was guarded twice over, by two
independent mechanisms rather than one.

Measured across the real run: notifications before, **2,315**. Notifications after, **2,315**.
Exactly unchanged, asserted inside the run and then independently re-read afterwards rather than
taken on trust.

### It was rehearsed before it was run

Before anything was written, a dry run scanned **9,272** messages across all eight mailboxes and
reported precisely what it intended to insert - no writes, no notifications, **zero errors**, and no
mailbox truncated. Only then was it run for real.

### One honest detail

Most of the recovered mail is from people who are not leads in our system yet. Those messages are
captured and stored, but they do not create cards on anybody's record, because there is nobody yet to
create them on. That is the design, not a miss - the wider book of people is a separate, scheduled
piece of work, and this mail will attach when they arrive.

## What you can do with it

| What happens | What you get |
|---|---|
| Someone emails one of our eight mailboxes | It is captured live and lands on their record |
| You open a lead who emailed us during the outage | Their history is there - it was replayed, not lost |
| A recovered message belongs to a known lead | It appears on their record exactly like a live one, because it went through the same door |
| A recovered message is from a stranger | It is stored, ready to attach the moment that person becomes a lead |
| The recovery is run again | Nothing is duplicated - each message is recognised as already stored |
