## The one sentence

**Everything on this page is lifted from what the website already renders — never re-drawn.** If you need a HouseLoop mark, a wordmark or a banner, take it from here. If what you need is not here, ask before you draw it: a hand-made lookalike is the thing this page exists to prevent.

<Note>
The mark and wordmark are real components in the app — `components/ui/HouseLoopLogo.tsx` and `components/ui/HouseLoopWordmark.tsx`. The images below are renders of those components, so they cannot drift from the site.
</Note>

## The lockup

The mark is a roofline over a loop, and it always sits **to the left** of the letters on a shared baseline. The wordmark is `House` in bold beside `Loop` in regular weight — the weight change is what separates the two words, never a space.

| Ground | Mark | `House` | `Loop` |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Light** | brand blue | navy | brand blue |
| **Dark, at display size** | brand blue | white | **brand blue** |
| **Dark, at UI size** | white | white | white, regular weight |

<Warning>
**The dark ground has two different treatments, and the discriminator is SIZE, not taste.**

At UI size — the site's own navy bands, where the mark is about 24px — the whole lockup goes white. Brand blue on navy is too dim to read that small, and `PageHeader.tsx` already made that call.

At display size — these banners, where the wordmark is 122px — the same blue carries easily, so the two-colour wordmark survives and `Loop` keeps its brand blue. **The rule was always about legibility at size; it was never a rule that blue may not touch navy.**

If you are unsure which you are making, you are making the UI size. Go all white.
</Warning>

## The tagline

> ## The Smart Way Home<span style={{ color: "#2D6DFF" }}>.</span>

**The period is brand blue and it is part of the wordmark, not punctuation.** Set it in the same size as the words and change only its colour. A tagline typeset without the blue period is not the tagline.

On the banners it is set in medium weight, a step lighter than the wordmark's bold — so the two read as a hierarchy rather than as two competing headlines.

## Colours

Only two colours carry the brand. Both are already tokens in the app — `app/globals.css`.

| Token | Hex | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `--hl-brand` | `#2D6DFF` | Brand blue. The mark, the `Loop`, the period, the primary button. |
| `--hl-navy` | `#0A1128` | Navy. Dark grounds, the footer, body type on white. |

<Warning>
**No green.** Green means the SMS channel or a real success state, nowhere else — see [Design Standards → Overview](/design/overview). And never paste a raw hex into app code: use the token.
</Warning>

## Typeface

**DM Sans**, the site's own face (`app/layout.tsx`, via `next/font/google`). The banners use 700 for the wordmark's `House`, 400 for `Loop`, and 500 for the tagline. Free from [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/DM+Sans).

## How these were made, and how to remake them

The source is a single HTML file — `docs-site/images/brand/banner-source.html` — rendered through real Chrome at LinkedIn's exact pixel dimensions. Every value in it is lifted from the app: the mark's own SVG path, the wordmark's weights, the two colour tokens, and DM Sans loaded as a webfont.

<Note>
**Chrome, not a command-line converter, and the reason is worth knowing.** `rsvg-convert` and ImageMagick were both available and both rejected: DM Sans is not installed as a system font on the build machine, so either would have quietly substituted a different face and shipped a wordmark that is not the brand's. Rendering through a browser that loads the real webfont is the only way the letters are certain to be right.
</Note>

To change a banner, edit the source, re-render at the same dimensions, and replace the file under `docs-site/images/brand/`. Keep the filenames — they carry their dimensions, which is what stops someone uploading a profile banner into a company cover slot.
