## 1 · What HouseLoop really is

**Every real-estate portal ever built is a marketplace of LISTINGS that sells your attention to
strangers. HouseLoop is a MEMORY of PEOPLE and their homes, with one mind that never forgets and
can actually do things.**

Zillow's product is the listing; the person is inventory — captured, scored, and sold to
whichever agent pays for the zip code. The relationship is severed at the handoff, every time,
by design. That severing is their business model, which is why they can never fix it.

HouseLoop inverts it. The unit of the product is not the house and not the lead — it is **the
RELATIONSHIP, held continuously across the whole life of a home**: the first anonymous search →
the registration → the conversations → the tours → the offer → the close → the years of owning
(the home-value pulse, the newsletter, the "should we sell?" question) → the next purchase → the
kids' first purchase. Nothing is ever handed off, because the thing on the other end — Myli — is
built ON the record, not beside it. Every message files on every person it touched. Every fact a
buyer mentions is captured forever. Every home has a permanent identity (the HLID). Every
teammate is a first-class profile in one operating system. One brain, one memory, one estate.

**The novel description, in one sentence:** *HouseLoop is the first real-estate company where the
software IS the agent's memory, the agent's memory IS the product, and the product never stops
working the relationship — from the first click to the last closing of their lives.*

What we are really building has three faces that are one thing:

- **For the CLIENT:** a ChatGPT that can actually buy you a house. She answers the PMI question
  like the best explainer you've ever read — then quotes the listing's *real* tax bill, captures
  your $20K-saved as a fact, reframes the $499K house against your real budget, offers John
  Nelson by name, books the tour for Saturday at 11, texts the showing partner, chases the
  feedback, and updates your search overnight. And she still remembers all of it two years later.
- **For THE TEAM:** an operating system disguised as a group text. The thread Zach, Ana, and
  Trish already live in becomes the product — with Myli inside it, parsing, filing, scheduling,
  and following up, while every word lands on the right lead's record and every teammate has a
  profile as rich as a lead's.
- **For THE BUSINESS:** a reactivation engine pointed at an asset no startup has — 49,000 real
  relationships with years of Zillow browsing history, 1,779 past clients with homes we can
  value monthly, and a licensed team ready to close what the machine warms up.

## 2 · The moat — why us, versus everyone

**Versus Zillow / Redfin:** they monetize the interruption; we monetize the continuation. Their
economics require selling the person to a stranger at the moment of highest intent — so their AI
can only ever be a receptionist for the auction. Ours keeps the person, which means our AI can be
the relationship. And our data compounds where theirs resets: browsing + conversation + captured
facts + transaction + home identity live in ONE estate (the seat model), so every interaction
makes the next one smarter. Zillow knows what you clicked. HouseLoop knows what you *said*, what
you *saved for a down payment*, what your wife thought of the kitchen on 93rd Street, and what
your house is worth this month.

**Versus ChatGPT:** four things it structurally cannot have — (1) the DATA: live MLS, the
listing's actual taxes, our metro's real numbers; (2) the MEMORY THAT FILES: its context
evaporates, our facts land on records that outlive the conversation; (3) the HANDS: it can
describe booking a tour, Myli books it — calendar, partner, confirmation, follow-up; (4) the
TEAM: on the other end of Myli is a real brokerage that shows the house and writes the offer.
ChatGPT is the world's best explainer with amnesia and no arms. Myli is the explainer with a
ledger, a calendar, a phone line, and a license behind her. *(And chat models are reactive and
time-blind — Zach's own insight. Myli is proactive and time-aware: tasks are her clock.)*

**The compounding moat nobody sees yet:** every conversation Myli has writes structured facts
into the estate. At 49K relationships that is a proprietary dataset about how real buyers in one
metro actually decide — which no portal (they don't converse) and no chatbot (it doesn't persist)
can replicate. The moat is not the AI. The moat is the RECORD the AI stands on, and it deepens
daily.

The mobile group thread is the TEAM's window onto the loop. The client dashboard is the
CLIENT's window onto the loop. The CRM is the RECORD of the loop. Myli is the loop's engine.
One estate underneath all four — which is why tonight's foundation work (seats, rooms, the
state stack) matters more than any single feature: it is the circle's axle. **The far-future GT
vision (every US home with a HouseLoop profile, vendors, HVAC scheduled by Myli, the insurance
claim she coordinates) is not a different product — it is the OWN segment of this same loop,
widened.** We do not have to pivot to reach it; we have to keep the circle turning.

## 4 · The architecture that does it (the spine in one breath)

Five layers, already law: **tapes** (verbatim capture — FUB, Zillow, pixel) → **staging** (one
classification) → **the core** (people + the one activity log + the seat model: every fact, a
seat for everyone it touched) → **reactions** (per-seat notification rules + catalog-keyed
triggers) → **projections** (every surface renders the same rows: the lead page, the thread, the
profile, the dashboard). Myli's engine rides the core: the **state stack** (what she's driving
toward, surviving every pivot), **playbooks** of drawn moves (every send visible and authorized
— the sever, the recipient band, the who-band), **tasks as her clock** (proactive, time-aware),
and the **unhobbled language layer** grounded in our knowledge base. Nothing speaks without a
drawn move; nothing files without a seat; nothing renders off a second copy. That is the whole
trick: **one record, one brain, many windows.**

## 5 · The reactivation play — the business engine

The asset: **49,250 FUB relationships** (98.7% measured importable-clean), **~30K with Zillow
browsing metadata**, **1,779 past clients** with valued homes, and the my-agent invite path
already written. The play, in ramps — never all at once, per the signed cohort law:

1. **PROVE (now → week 4):** the 100-lead cohort. Myli handles them end-to-end on the proven
   roads. The simulator rehearses every scenario before a real human sees it.
2. **THE WARM BOOK (weeks 3–8):** the 1,779 past clients get their home-value dashboards
   provisioned + the monthly value pulse + Myli's check-ins. These people already love us —
   they are the lowest-risk, highest-trust reactivation and the referral engine.
3. **THE BROWSING-SEEDED RAMP (weeks 6–12):** cohorts from the 30K with Zillow metadata,
   personalized from what we already know: *"You were looking at 4-beds in Olathe last spring —
   want to see what they're going for now? I put together your home search dashboard."* Email +
   text through the gates, each cohort measured before the next widens.
4. **THE FULL BOOK (months 3–6):** the remaining ~19K, the my-agent invites, the newsletter as
   the standing heartbeat. Every reactivated person lands on a dashboard where Myli is already
   mid-conversation with them.

**The funnel honesty:** 3–4 closings/month by month 6 means roughly 8–12 active transactions,
which at conservative reactivation numbers means we need **~2,000–3,000 people genuinely active
on dashboards by month 4–5** — which the ramp above is sized to produce. 8–10/month by months
9–12 is the same funnel at full-book scale plus the referral flywheel from segment OWN. The
levers if we run behind: widen cohorts faster, deepen the newsletter, and lean on the past-client
book, which converts at multiples of cold reactivation.

## 7 · Unknown unknowns — and how we account for what we can't name

We cannot list them (that's the definition), so we build the machine that CATCHES them:

1. **The receipts culture** — nothing is true without a measurement; tonight alone it overturned
   the desk four times. Unknown unknowns die on contact with instruments.
2. **The simulator** — every scenario rehearsed against the real engine before a real human sees
   it. The cheapest place in the company to be wrong.
3. **Cohort exposure** — 40 → 100 → the book. An unknown that surfaces at 100 leads costs an
   apology; at 49K it costs the company. The ramp IS the insurance.
4. **The no-lies harness + the cages** — a defect cannot ship silently; a kind cannot fire
   unproven; a send cannot leave ungated.
5. **Myli's escalation default** — when she doesn't know, she says so and hands to a human. The
   unknown unknowns of AI conversation land on a licensed person, not on a lead.
6. **The widening reviews** — a human (Zach) at every ramp gate, reading transcripts, with the
   copy desk on every new surface's first output.

The named known-unknowns, tracked openly: carrier/group-MMS behavior at scale · LLM conversation
quality drift at volume · reactivation response rates (we hold priors, not data — the first
cohorts EXIST to price this) · Zillow conduit policy risk (mitigated: the estate is ours, FUB is
a pipe, D2 says so) · a 49K book's deliverability reputation (warmed by the cohort ramp).
