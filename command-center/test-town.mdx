## The inboxes

Two real Google Workspace mailboxes, IMAP on, passwords in root `.env`.

| Mailbox | Env keys | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **qa-probe@houseloop.ai** | `QA_PROBE_EMAIL` / `QA_PROBE_PASSWORD` | every persona above, via plus-addressing |
| **qa-agent@houseloop.ai** | `QA_AGENT_EMAIL` / `QA_AGENT_PASSWORD` | the test AGENT seat — receives the real Gmail calendar invite |

**Plus-addressing is the whole trick.** `qa-probe+lender@` and `qa-probe+tc@` are distinct addresses
to the CRM and to Gmail's filters, but they all deliver to the ONE `qa-probe` mailbox. A walker reads
five personas' mail with one IMAP login and zero extra Workspace seats.

## ⚠ The own-line ceiling — what these lines CANNOT prove

Measured 8/11, not assumed: **all three candidate numbers are rows in `hl_phone_numbers`**, so
`isOwnLine()` (`lib/phone/own-number.ts`) returns TRUE for every one of them. That guard exists for a
good reason — our own notification line once tripped three "a stranger texted you" alerts on Zach's
phone — but it puts a ceiling on the town.

Tracing `app/api/phone/webhooks/sms/route.ts` with `fromIsOurs = true`:

**Still works** (these are not behind the guard) — `findLeadByPhone` resolves the sender to the
persona, so an inbound text threads onto the persona's lead, `logMessage` writes the canonical
`hl_messages` row, the SSE broker pushes it live, and the **lead-owner notification fires**. The
lifeline lane is fully walkable.

**Suppressed** — three things sit behind `if (!fromIsOurs)` and cannot be walked with these lines:

1. **inbox-subscriber pings** on an inbound text (`notifyInboxSubscribers`),
2. **auto-mint on an unknown sender** (`mintPhoneLead` — fail-closed by design),
3. the **lead-less trigger event** and the owner-aware stranger alert (`fireInboxTrigger`,
   `notifyInboundOwnerAware`).

**To close it,** the town needs one line that is deliberately NOT in `hl_phone_numbers` — either a
fresh purchase left unregistered, or the "Available" row (label "Available", NULL category — it reads
like a placeholder someone left behind, not a configured line) deleted so +1 913-733-3704 looks like
an outsider. That is a Zach decision; both options are one row.

## The rules

1. **Personas are PARKED, always.** Every one sits on the `Incoming` disposition (`behavior='hide'`).
   The leads working view keeps only `behavior='none'` (`lib/leads-query.ts:658`), so a persona can
   never surface in Zach's list. Measured after seeding: **0 of 5 visible in the working view.**
2. **Never the DNC disposition.** The activity harness parks its probe on DNC (highest-position hide).
   The town takes the LOWEST-position hide instead, because a persona whose entire job is receiving
   texts and emails must not carry a label that will one day be read as an instruction.
3. **Never text or email a real person from a QA flow.** Every endpoint in this town is ours. If a
   walk needs a recipient the town does not have, add the persona here first — do not borrow a real lead.
4. **Plus-addressing all lands in `qa-probe`.** Adding a persona means adding a `qa-probe+NAME@` row
   to `TOWN` in `tools/qa/test-town.mjs`; no mailbox provisioning is required.
5. **Seeding never fires a workflow.** The seeder posts `fireWorkflow: false`. The town is where we
   fire things deliberately; being born is not one of them.
6. **The seeder never re-parks a persona that is already parked** — a re-PATCH would log a
   `lead_dispositioned` activity on every run and bury the town's real history under seeding noise.

## Findings from the seeding walk (reported, not fixed)

**The actor standard passes.** All 10 activity rows the seeding produced carry a NAMED actor
(`by_name = 'Zach Spradling'`, `by_kind = 'user'`, `actor_type = 'human'`). **Zero "An agent…" cards.**

Three things are worth someone's attention:

1. **Four of five personas have no creation card.** A lead born through the connected-people door gets
   `connected_person_added` logged on the ANCHOR's feed and **nothing at all on its own**. Quincy has a
   `lead_created` row; Quinn, QA Lender, QA TC and QA Showing Partner each have exactly one activity —
   their disposition park. Four real leads exist with no record of their own birth. Under
   ALL_FEED_IS_EVERYTHING that is an incomplete build, and it applies to every spouse and counterparty
   a human has ever added by hand, not just to the town.
2. **`connected_person_added` does not stamp `source`.** It writes NULL where `lead_created` and
   `lead_dispositioned` both write `'crm'`.
3. **The connection-type vocabulary does not exist.** `lib/leads/relationships.ts` documents a table
   `hl_connected_people_type` (mig 0638) holding spouse · partner · family · business_partner · friend ·
   llc · trust · relocation · estate · other — **that table is not in the CRM database.**
   `hl_connected_people.connection_type` is free `text` with no FK and a `'household'` default, and the
   Add-Connected-Person modal offers a free-text box plus seven chips: Spouse, Wife, Husband, Sister,
   Brother, Daughter, Son. **All seven are family.** A lender, a TC, or a showing partner has no
   first-class type — so the town's counterparties carry the free-text labels `Lender`,
   `Transaction Coordinator`, `Showing Partner`. No enum value was invented, because there is no enum.
   Whether service professionals belong in the same vocabulary as a spouse is a design question
   (item #74's territory), not something to settle in a seeding script.
