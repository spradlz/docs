## 2 · THE WHY — what is actually wrong, in plain English

**One sentence:** *Five different places can each decide the same fact about your customer, and when the system
does not know something, some layer makes an answer up rather than saying so.*

ChatGPT, who chartered and judged this audit, named it in two halves, and both halves are load-bearing:

> **DISTRIBUTED AUTHORITY plus UNREPRESENTED UNCERTAINTY.**

**DISTRIBUTED AUTHORITY — five places can answer one question.** Nothing is *broken* in any one of them. Each
was written by someone sensible, each works on its own, and each is entitled to decide the same thing. So the
CRM and the website can hold two different values for the same house (**F1**: 1,875 of 1,920 past-client homes
disagree, median gap $4,723, worst $1.9M). Two tables can hold two different medians for the same ZIP
(**F2**: 132 of 140 ZIPs disagree). That is not a bug you can find, because there is no wrong line of code —
**there are two right ones**, and the customer sees whichever loaded first.

**UNREPRESENTED UNCERTAINTY — the system cannot say "I don't know."** When a value is missing, stale, or
conflicted, there is nowhere in the data to record *that*, so something downstream fills the hole with a
number, a confident sentence, or a green check. This is why a report can read "6 of 6 confirmed" over a
manufactured fact, and why a tour can tell a customer it failed when it did not.

**Why it feels like a memory problem.** It is the same disease seen from the customer's chair: if the place
that knows who someone is and the place that stores what they said are two different places keyed differently,
she is not forgetting — **she is asking the wrong filing cabinet.**

### 2.1 · The cure, in one line
**One authority per question.** For every question Myli can answer, exactly one component is entitled to decide
it; everything else reads a projection of that answer. Plus: a value that can be unknown must be able to *say*
it is unknown, all the way to the screen.

## 3 · WHAT MYLI IS

The Myli OS is everything Myli is and does, as one build. A stranger lands on HouseLoop, talks to her, and she
answers from real data with her own words. They register and become a real person in the CRM. Every fact she
heard becomes a row on that person. Weeks later she texts them and remembers all of it. The Myli OS is that
whole loop plus the machinery underneath it: one place that knows what happened in a conversation, one place
that knows who this person is, one place that counts the homes, one place that decides what she is allowed to
offer. Today there are several places for each of those, which is why she contradicts herself and forgets
things. The work is not building more; it is getting down to one of each and deleting the rest.
