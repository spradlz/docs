## Ways to Make Your Offer Stronger in a Competitive Market

When multiple buyers are interested in the same home, there are several ways to make your offer more
attractive to the seller. Here are the main options:

### 1. Closing Date

A typical financed purchase closes in about 30 days. Cash offers, however, often close in 7–10 days, which
sellers usually prefer.

To compete with cash offers, we can work with your lender to have your loan fully underwritten before we
find a house, rather than just getting pre-approved. By submitting all of your financial documentation in
advance, we may be able to close in as little as 10 days instead of the traditional 30.

### 2. Inspection Options

**Option 1 – Traditional Inspection**

- 10-day inspection period.
- We inspect the home and can request repairs or credits.
- Typically, there are an additional 5 days to negotiate any inspection items.

**Option 2 – "As-Is" with Inspection**

- You agree upfront that you will not ask the seller for any repairs or credits.
- You still have the right to complete inspections for your own knowledge.
- If the inspection reveals something you are not comfortable with, you still have the right to cancel the
  contract during the inspection period.

**Option 3 – Waive Inspections**

- You purchase the home completely as-is.
- No inspections are performed.
- This is the strongest inspection option but also carries the most risk.

### 3. Escalation Clause

Instead of simply offering one price, you can include an escalation clause.

For example:

- Offer to pay $15,700 above the highest competing offer, up to a maximum purchase price that you are
  comfortable with.

Generally:

- Escalating lower than $2,500–$5,000 above another offer may not be enough in a very competitive market.
- In many multiple-offer situations, buyers often need to be $15,000–$25,000 above asking price to make a
  meaningful difference.
- We also like to use non-round numbers (such as $15,700 instead of $15,000), since they can make your
  offer stand out.

### 4. Earnest Money Deposit

A typical earnest money deposit is about 1% of the purchase price.

Normally:

- The earnest money is usually credited back to you at closing.
- If the contract is canceled under a contingency, the funds are generally returned according to the
  contract terms, but never automatically back.
- If there is a dispute, both parties must sign a release instructing the title company how to disburse the
  funds. If the parties cannot agree, the dispute may require mediation or legal resolution.

One way to make your offer stronger is to make the earnest money non-refundable (or partially
non-refundable) after certain contingencies are removed. This gives the seller additional confidence that
you are committed to purchasing the home.

If you complete the purchase, the earnest money is still fully credited toward your purchase at closing.

### 5. Appraisal Gap Coverage

If the home appraises for less than the purchase price, the lender will generally base the loan on the
appraised value, not the contract price.

To strengthen your offer, you can agree to cover the difference between the appraised value and the
purchase price.

Examples:

- Cover the entire appraisal gap, or
- Cover up to a specific amount, such as $5,000, $10,000, or another amount you choose.

Some loan programs may not require an appraisal in certain situations, but that depends on the lender and
loan type.

### 6. Closing Costs and Prepaid Expenses

To make your offer more competitive, buyers can agree to pay their own closing costs and prepaid expenses,
rather than asking the seller to contribute toward them.

### 7. Seller's Closing Costs

In some situations, buyers may even offer to contribute toward the seller's closing costs.

A common range is approximately:

- $1,500–$5,000, depending on the situation.

This can help offset some of the seller's expenses, such as title fees or other transaction-related costs,
and may make your offer more appealing.

### Summary

The strongest offers typically combine several of these strategies, such as:

- ✅ Faster closing (10 days when possible)
- ✅ "As-is" inspection terms
- ✅ Escalation clause
- ✅ Larger or non-refundable earnest money deposit
- ✅ Appraisal gap coverage
- ✅ Buyer pays their own closing costs
- ✅ Buyer contributes toward seller's closing costs (when appropriate)

Not every strategy is right for every buyer. Our job is to help you decide which options fit your comfort.
