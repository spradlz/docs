Every geo page on houseloop.ai renders from **one component stack**, not per-route JSX. This page is the
canonical description of that shape and of the September 2026 retirement of its previous generation.

## The stack

A geo route is a thin shell over the shared `SearchResultsPage`. The SEO block below the results is
`components/geo/LocationSeo.tsx`, which resolves the location once and composes:

1. **Breadcrumb** — `BreadcrumbList` JSON-LD alongside it.
2. **`GeoSeoContent`** — market trends · the welcome article · schools · the sixteen live-number FAQs
   (`FAQPage` JSON-LD, rendered expanded).
3. **`GeoPageBody`** — the overview prose · the counted facet bank · the listing-overview tiles.
4. **`WeeklyMovers`** — under contract and newly listed, when the geo has them.
5. **`GeoDirectory`** — the two market tables (below).

Sections render in **push order**. There is no re-sorting step.

## The two market tables

`GeoDirectory` renders labelled sections. Two survive, both as rich market tables reading
`hl_geo_market_stats`:

- **Property types in {Place}** — one row per home type that has homes for sale.
- **Neighborhood Market Statistics in {Place}** — one row per subdivision in the nightly cache.

### The 12-row cap

The neighbourhood table is capped at **12 rows** with a `Show all N ▾` pill
(`components/geo/MarketTableShelf.tsx`). Overland Park has 249 qualifying subdivisions.

The cap is a **real server-side slice**, not a CSS hide, and that distinction is the whole point:

| | before the cap | after |
|---|---|---|
| Anchors on the Overland Park page | 1,567 | **242** |
| From this one table | 1,225 (78%) | 60 |
| `?status=` parameter URLs | 735 | **36** |

Those `?status=closed\|pending\|comingSoon` variants self-canonicalise to the clean neighbourhood URL,
so they were never a duplicate-indexing problem — they were a **crawl-budget** problem. A canonical
stops indexing; it does not stop fetching.

<Note>
The home page's `ShowMoreGrid` shelf looks identical and does the **opposite**: it renders every item
and hides the overflow, because that tail's job is to put all 134 city links in the HTML for a crawler.
Do not copy one to the other. The rows past this cap still ship as inert JSON in the RSC payload (that
is how the pill expands without a round trip) — what the slice removes is the **anchors**.
</Note>

## Titles are built from the geo's own level

A section title is never concatenated onto a bare place name. `${place}` resolves to
`neighborhood ?? city ?? county ?? state`, which produced **"Property Types in Johnson"** on a county
page and **"Leawood Estates Neighborhood Homes"** on a neighbourhood page.

Titles now use `locationLabel()` and branch on level:

| Level | Neighbourhood table title |
|---|---|
| City | `Neighborhood Market Statistics in Overland Park, KS` |
| County | `Neighborhood Market Statistics in Johnson County, KS` |
| Zip | `Neighborhood Market Statistics in Overland Park, KS` — the **city**, because the rows are the city's subdivisions and the feed has no neighbourhood↔zip map |
| Neighbourhood | `Nearby Neighborhood Market Statistics` |

## What was retired

Six sections were rendering **below** the current shape rather than being replaced by it, so every geo
page shipped two generations at once — 15 `<h2>`s where the shape draws 9. All are gone:

- Find a Home You'll Love in {Place}
- Popular Searches in {Place}
- More to Explore in {Place}
- Explore Nearby Cities
- {Place} Homes by Zip Code
- Estimate Your Home Sale Proceeds

Their successors are the property-type, feature and nearby chip rows. `orderSections()` and the
`SECTION_ORDER` array went with them.

## Verifying a change

```bash
curl -s http://localhost:3001/kansas/johnson-county/overland-park \
  | grep -o '<h2[^>]*>' | wc -l          # 9 on a city page
npx tsc --noEmit -p apps/houseloop-web    # per-route builds hide type errors behind a 200
```

A 200 on the home page does not prove the tree type-checks — Next compiles per route.
