## The vision, in one paragraph

The team already works in a group text. A lead asks to tour a house, Zach texts the thread that
has Trish (the showing partner) and Ana (his wife, who writes the offers) in it, Trish says yes or
no, the showing gets scheduled, and afterwards Trish drops her feedback into that same thread —
what the client liked, what they didn't, whether they want a saved search. The MVP is to **move
that thread into the HouseLoop app and put Myli inside it**, so the coordination that already
happens produces CRM records as a side effect instead of as a chore. In Zach's words: *"Whenever I
open the HouseLoop mobile app it basically looks like my text threads,"* and *"Myli's in the text
thread, Myli's listening, Myli's extracting all of the facts, updating Lucy's facts, setting next
follow-up tasks."* The app is his threads; Myli is a participant in them, not a panel beside them.

## Why this lives INSIDE HouseLoop, not beside it

The obvious objection to this MVP is "you're building a chat app." The answer is that the group
thread is not a chat app bolted onto the CRM — **it is the estate's own conversation surface**, and
three already-signed decisions are what make that true rather than a slogan.

**One master estate; teams are seats (D1).** HouseLoop owns every person and every lead forever;
teams get routing and visibility, not ownership. A team leaving never takes leads, and **Myli keeps
the relationship.** That is what makes a thread between Zach, Trish and Ana part of the record
instead of a private side-channel: Trish is a *seat on the estate*, so the conversation she has
about Dana Whitfield belongs to Dana's record whether Trish stays with the team or not. Put the same
conversation in iMessage or Slack and it belongs to the phone, and it leaves when the person does.

**Myli is one brain (locked 2026-07-21, constitutional).** One persona, one per-lead working memory,
one rulebook, sitting above the models — the models are swappable per-channel language engines.
Zach, signed 8/23: *"It's always one brain. She's never stale. She owns the relationship."* This is
what makes Myli's presence in the thread worth anything. She is not a bot listening to a channel and
writing notes into a separate system; she is reading and writing the **same** per-lead memory that
the lead page, the website follow-ups and the voice lane read. Which is why, in the direct thread,
she can answer "did Trish get the offer written?" without anyone opening the lead — and why what
Trish says in the group thread changes what Myli says to Dana an hour later.

**The seat model is why one message can be in four places without being copied.** One fact, one
actor, and a seat for every person it touched. The group message lives on the lead's record, in the
thread, and on Zach's, Trish's and Ana's profiles **as the same row read four ways** — not four
copies that drift. Copies are what produced the 8/14 defect this law was written from.

Together those three make the pitch a single line: **everything said in the thread becomes part of
the record Myli works from.** Zach the same night: *"Myli's running everything behind the scenes and
just updating the user."* A conversation surface that lives outside the estate cannot do that,
because the coordination and the record would be two different things — which is exactly the chore
this MVP exists to delete.

## The MVP cut, in Zach's own words

These five lines are the whole scope. Anything not on this list is a later slice.

| # | The cut | Zach, verbatim |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One group thread: Zach · Trish · Ana · Myli** | "a group text happening between my wife, Trish, Myli and myself" |
| 2 | **Trish texts through the app — no phone number for her** | "**Trish doesn't even need a Twilio number** — for the minimum viable product, Trish just texts through the mobile app, and Myli is in the text thread." |
| 3 | **A user detail page for Trish, and one for Zach** | "I need a Trish profile where I can see all of Trish's texts and appointments, and I need my own personal user detail page." |
| 4 | **Myli in the thread: extracting facts, asking after a showing** | "I need to be able to give feedback in that thread, schedule in that thread, talk back and forth — and Myli's there to extract facts, communicate anything coming up, and ask questions after a showing." |
| 5 | **A direct thread with Myli, per user** | "I can have a text thread specifically with Myli: 'hey, what's going on with Lucy Hartman? Did Trish get the offer written?'" |

The point of cut 2 is worth saying plainly, because it decides the build: **for the MVP the
teammate channel is in-app only.** No SMS to Trish, no number to provision, no carrier
registration. Client threads keep going out over Twilio exactly as they do today.

## The architecture it rides

Nothing here needs a new architecture. Every piece has already been signed; this MVP is the first
product that consumes them together.

### The seat model is the engine

[The seat model](/schema-lock/overview) (signed 2026-08-14) says: **every event is one FACT
with one ACTOR and a SEAT for every person it touched, and every surface reads the seats instead of
re-deriving who did what.** That is exactly what Zach asked for as a test outcome:

> "all of that shows up in one group text message in the mobile app — **and it shows up in the
> thread of the three user profiles attached to that**" [and the lead].

One message posted to the group thread produces **one fact** and a seat for each party it touched:
the thread, the lead it is about, and Zach, Trish, Ana and Myli individually. The thread screen,
the lead's activity log, and all three user detail pages then render **the same rows** — so they
cannot disagree. This is the fix for the 8/14 defect the law was written from, where a group email
was filed on one lead through one foreign key and every surface lied about it.

Two consequences the build has to honour:

- **The FROM seat is the actor, forever.** In a four-person thread the sender is never re-derived
  from thread ownership or lead ownership. Myli's messages are Myli's; Trish's are Trish's.
- **A group thread about two leads files on both.** Conner and Jasmeet are one thread and two lead
  records; one message lands on both, idempotently, through `logActivity` — the one door.

### The in-app channel arrives DENY-BY-DEFAULT for AI

The MVP adds a **new send channel** (in-app messages between HouseLoop users).
[`AI_CONTAINMENT_LAW`](/command-center/ai-containment-law) is unambiguous about what that means:

> "A NEW send channel is DENY-BY-DEFAULT for AI. It must be structurally impossible to add a
> transport an AI can reach without routing through the gate."

So the in-app channel is built as a transport behind the **one** `deliver()` facade, and Myli's
posts into the group thread pass the same central authorization gate every other AI send passes —
master switch, AI comms enabled, the specific AI enabled, the channel enabled, a real
`playbook_execution_id`, a real visible `step_id`, business hours, suppressions. A failed check is
a **REJECT**, never a downgrade-and-send.

And per the same law's 8/12 amendment, **an AI message to a team member is still an AI message**:
Myli asking Trish for her availability is AI communication and is killable by the comms master
switch, even though Trish is on the team and not a client. The discriminator is `composed_by`, not
recipient.

### NEGOTIATOR is a new fact

> "Group text between anyone that's attached to a lead — think showing partner, agent, and
> NEGOTIATOR, which my wife would be — **a negotiator on a lead, that's a new role we don't have**."

Showing partner, lender and TC already exist as lead collaborator roles. **Negotiator does not.**
It is a new role row, and it is the role that decides who gets seated on an offer conversation
later. Adding it is a schema change and therefore a desk-signed step, not a drive-by.

### The user-profile estate reuses the lead detail page

> "I would have a **Trish profile that looks just like a lead's profile**. We've got lead profiles
> and we've got user profiles — lead detail pages and user detail pages."

Under [`REUSE_NOT_RECREATE`](/claude-process/overview) that sentence is a build instruction, not a
metaphor. The user detail page is **the lead detail page in a user mode** — the same three lanes,
the same `LeadLane` / `CenterLane` / `MyliLane` components, the same section headers and lane
chrome. There is no `UserLane`, no `UserDetail`, no lookalike. Left lane: name, cell, email, role,
upcoming appointments top to bottom, tasks top to bottom. Middle: the communication view, newest
first, with the team group thread present. Right: Myli.

## The MVP test script

This is Zach's own test, written as steps. It is the acceptance criteria: if this runs clean end to
end, the MVP is real; if any one line fails, it isn't.

1. **Myli posts to the group thread** asking Trish for availability on a real tour request —
   "Hey Trish! My client Dana Whitfield would love to see 8133 W 93rd St in Overland Park on
   Saturday at 11:00 — does that work for you to show it?"
2. **Trish replies in the app** — no SMS, no Twilio number involved.
3. **The reply appears in the group thread** for Zach and Ana, in order, attributed to Trish.
4. **The same message appears on the lead's record** — the activity log for Dana Whitfield.
5. **The same message appears on all three user detail pages** — Zach's, Trish's, and Ana's
   communication view.
6. **Myli's post carries its authorization** — a real execution id and a real visible step; with
   the AI comms master switched off, step 1 does not send at all.
7. **After the showing, Trish's feedback produces records** — Myli extracts the facts onto the
   lead, creates the saved search she was asked for, and sets the follow-up task, each one visible
   as a receipt in the thread.

Steps 3–5 are one assertion in three places on purpose: they are the seat model's whole claim, and
they are the thing that was broken on 8/14.

## Open decisions — these block the lock

The wireframe names these; they are repeated here so they don't get lost in a picture.

1. **Is the team group thread one standing thread, or one per lead?** The source says both:
   *"Every lead would have its own thread"* and *"a group text happening between my wife, Trish,
   Myli and myself."* The draft wireframe shows **one standing team thread** carrying an @lead band
   that changes with the subject. A thread-per-lead is an equally faithful reading and a materially
   different app. **This answer comes first; most of the rest depends on it.**
2. **Threads or Inbox?** The shipped app lands on **Inbox** (locked 7/26). The draft names the
   landing tab **Threads**, from Zach's words. Both cannot be the name.
3. **Do teammate and client rows look identical in the list?** They are different transports
   (in-app vs Twilio SMS). The draft deliberately makes them look the same, because that is what
   "it looks like my text threads" means — but it is a choice worth confirming.
4. **Trish's real user record** — surname, cell, email are placeholders in the wireframe on
   purpose; no invented contact details get ported onto a real person's profile.
