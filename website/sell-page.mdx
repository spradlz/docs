The `/sell` page is where an owner asks what their home is worth and how quickly it would move. The
value half is documented in [HouseLoop Home Value](/guides/houseloop-home-value). This page covers
the market half — the demand report — because the numbers it prints are easy to confuse with each
other and were, until 2026-09-08, wrong.

## One "days" number per surface, and they are not the same fact

Two different "days" figures exist in the product. Only one appears on any given page, and that is
deliberate — printing both together reads as a contradiction rather than as two facts.

| | what it measures | where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| **Median days to contract** | how long homes that actually *sold* in the last 12 months took to go under contract | the geo stat tile, the geo FAQ, the demand report |
| **Days on market** | how long *this one listing* has been sitting | the listing card only |

Days to contract is what a seller means by "how long will this take", so it is the number every
market-level surface shows. The other figure describes a single home's own age, which is useful on
that home's card and misleading as a market statistic: it only counts homes that have **not** sold,
so it runs high for the same reason a queue of waiting customers looks longer than the average wait.

Aggregated over a place the gap is wide — Overland Park's active inventory averages **72 days** while
its homes go under contract in a median of **16** — which is why the aggregate is not printed beside
the other.

The metro median days to contract is **22**. A place with fewer than **5** sales in 12 months shows a
dash and its FAQ sentence is omitted entirely, because a median over four homes ranges from 0 to
1,058 days across the board.

## The demand verdict is relative to the metro

| | old rule | new rule (2026-09-08) |
|---|---|---|
| input | average of the feed's `daysOnMarket` over **active** inventory | median days to contract over homes **sold** in 12 months |
| high demand | under 15 days, absolute | at least 25% faster than the metro |
| low demand | over 45 days, absolute | at least 25% slower than the metro |
| steady | — | between the two |

Both halves of the old rule were wrong, and they hid each other. The feed's column reads
systematically low, so the absolute thresholds had been quietly calibrated to it and the page leaned
toward "high demand". Correcting only the number would have swung every market to "low" instead,
because no honest absolute threshold exists — 22 days is fast in one metro and slow in another.

Measured across the board the day this shipped, the relative rule gives **33 high, 71 steady, 176
low**. That spread is the point: a verdict that returns the same answer everywhere is not a verdict.

When a place has no honest days-to-contract figure, the days signal **abstains** and sell-to-list
price is the only voice. It never falls back to "high".

## Where the numbers come from

`getGeoStats` computes all three figures and gates the thin-sample rule in one place, so every
consumer inherits it. The per-listing days figure is derived from the MLS listed date and never from the feed's
`daysOnMarket` column — the reasoning, and the measurement behind it, is in
[Cards & pill rules](/guides/houseloop-cards-and-pill-rules).

`lib/geo/geo-stats.probe.ts` is the check that keeps these honest: it compares every rendered figure
against an independent query of the same fact. It exists because all three of these surfaces agreed
with each other before the fix and all three were wrong — they agreed by sharing one bad column, and
no consistency check can see that.
